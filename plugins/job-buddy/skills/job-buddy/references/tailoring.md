# Tailoring CVs and cover letters

## The honesty line

Tailoring means choosing, ordering and phrasing true things so the most relevant ones are
easiest to see. It never means adding things that aren't true.

Allowed:
- Reordering sections and bullets so the most relevant experience comes first
- Rewriting a bullet to use the JD's terminology for something they genuinely did
  (e.g. their CV says "built dashboards", the JD says "reporting and analytics"; that's the
  same work, so use the JD's words)
- Adding a short profile/summary line aimed at this role
- Surfacing a skill or tool they've told you they used but left off the CV, **only if they
  confirmed it** in this or a previous conversation (record it in profile.md under
  "Extra experience confirmed by me" so it's reusable)
- Trimming irrelevant material to keep length right

Not allowed:
- Skills, tools, credentials, titles, employers or dates they don't have
- Metrics that aren't in the CV or confirmed by them (don't upgrade "improved" to "improved 40%")
- Implying seniority or ownership they didn't have ("led" when they "supported")

If the JD asks for something they don't have, leave it out of the CV, list it as a gap in your
summary to the user, and, if it helps, suggest how a cover letter could address it honestly
(adjacent experience, how quickly they'd pick it up).

Do not chase a keyword-match percentage. Applicant tracking systems vary enormously, and
stuffing keywords makes the CV worse for the human who reads it next. Use the JD's language
where it truthfully fits, and stop there.

## Which CV to start from

Always start from the master CV of the track the role was scored under (profile.md lists it
as "Master CV"). Never mix bullets from another track's CV unless the user confirms they
are true and relevant.

## Format

- Follow the conventions in the profile's "Tailoring preferences" if set.
- Defaults for Ireland / UK: 2 pages max, no photo, no date of birth, a short profile at the top,
  reverse-chronological experience, simple single-column layout (no tables, text boxes or
  graphics, which some applicant tracking systems can't read).
- Keep their contact details and section order consistent with their master CV unless they
  ask otherwise.
- File names: `<Name> - CV - <Company>.docx` and `<Name> - Cover letter - <Company>.docx`.

## Sounding like them, not like AI

Recruiters skim, and machine-sounding CVs get skimmed faster. Every CV and letter should read
as if the user wrote it on a good day.

- Start from their own phrasing in the master CV. Rewrite only what needs to change for this
  role; don't launder the whole document.
- Avoid stock phrases: "spearheaded", "leveraged", "results-driven", "dynamic", "passionate
  about", "proven track record", "synergy", "cutting-edge", "seamlessly", "utilise" (say "use"),
  and piles of adjectives before a noun.
- Vary bullet length and shape. Not every bullet needs to be verb + metric + impact.
- Concrete beats grand: say what they built, ran, fixed or delivered, in plain words.
- Before finishing, reread specifically for anything that sounds templated and rewrite it.

## Cover letters

- Under 300 words, addressed to a named person if the listing gives one.
- No "I am writing to express my interest in…" opener. Start with something specific: why this
  organisation or this role, based on something real (a project, a recent announcement, the
  team's remit). A quick web search is fine; cite nothing you can't verify.
- Middle: two or three of their real achievements mapped to the role's main needs.
- If there's an obvious gap the reader will notice, address it briefly and honestly.
- Close with availability (notice period, start date) if the profile has it.
- Match the formality of their field: a hospital research post and a startup ops role should
  not sound the same.

## After writing

Tell the user in 3–5 lines:
- what you emphasised and why,
- what you cut,
- gaps you did not paper over,
- anything you'd like them to double-check (a phrasing that stretches what they told you, a
  date you inferred).
