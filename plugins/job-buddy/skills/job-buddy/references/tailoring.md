# Tailoring CVs and cover letters

## The honesty line

Tailoring means choosing, ordering and phrasing true things so the most relevant ones are
easiest to see. It never means adding things that aren't true.

Allowed:
- Reordering sections and bullets so the most relevant experience comes first
- Rewriting a bullet to use the JD's terminology for something they genuinely did
  (e.g. their CV says "built dashboards", the JD says "reporting and analytics"; that's the
  same work, so use the JD's words)
- Adding a short profile/summary line aimed at this role
- Surfacing a skill or tool they've told you they used but left off the CV, **only if they
  confirmed it** in this or a previous conversation (record it in profile.md under
  "Extra experience confirmed by me" so it's reusable)
- Trimming irrelevant material to keep length right

Not allowed:
- Skills, tools, credentials, titles, employers or dates they don't have
- Metrics that aren't in the CV or confirmed by them (don't upgrade "improved" to "improved 40%")
- Numbers you work out yourself, such as "7 years' experience" added up from job dates, or a
  team size or percentage inferred from context. Dates are often approximate, so a derived
  total can be wrong. Use the CV's own wording, or ask them to confirm the figure first.
- Stretching a claim past what the CV says: who uses their work ("reporting both teams
  use"), what a workflow connects to, or its scope. If a stronger version would fit the ad
  better, ask whether it's true instead of writing it.
- Implying seniority or ownership they didn't have ("led" when they "supported")

These hold even when the user asks for "measurable achievements for every bullet" or a
template prompt says to: a bullet with no real number gets a concrete outcome in words, never
an invented figure. Say so briefly if they asked for numbers you couldn't use.

Before handing over, reread every line of the tailored text against the CV: each claim, number
and name must be traceable to the CV or to something they confirmed.

## Which CV to start from

Always start from the master CV of the track the role was scored under (profile.md lists it
as "Master CV"). Never mix bullets from another track's CV unless the user confirms they
are true and relevant.

## Making bullets stronger, honestly

- **Duties become outcomes.** "Responsible for Salesforce admin" says what the job was;
  "Ran Salesforce for 40 reps and rebuilt lead routing" says what they did. Rewrite duties as
  what they built, ran, fixed, changed or delivered, using only facts already in the CV.
- **Result first, where there is a real result.** "Cut lead response from 2 days to 4 hours
  by rebuilding routing in HubSpot" beats the action-first version. Where there's no result,
  don't fake the shape; a plain, concrete action is fine.
- **Ask for the numbers instead of inventing them (the numbers interview).** Before writing,
  pick the two to four bullets that would be much stronger with a figure, and ask about each
  in one short, specific question: "Roughly how many reps used the routing you built?", "Do
  you know how much faster lead response got?". Accept "about" figures they're comfortable
  defending in an interview, and "don't know" without pressing. Save every answer under
  "Extra experience confirmed by me" in profile.md so later CVs reuse it without asking again.
  If they'd rather not answer now, write the outcome in words and move on.

## Tailor to the kind of employer, not only the ad

The same true facts lean differently by employer. From the ad and anything verifiable about
the company, place it and adjust emphasis:
- **Startup or small team**: built from scratch, range across jobs, speed, ownership.
- **Scale-up**: building process as things grow, systems that scale, cross-team work.
- **Large company or multinational**: scale, process, stakeholders, governance, reliability.
- **Public sector, health, education, charity**: service to the public or patients, standards
  and compliance, the competency framework the posting names (mirror its headings).
Change emphasis and order, never the facts.

## Length and format

Page length (unless profile.md's tailoring notes set something else):
- **Under 3 years' experience: 1 page.** Up to 1.5 pages only for real substance (research,
  publications, several relevant projects) and only if they agree.
- **3–6 years: 1 page by default**; up to 1.5 when the role needs the extra detail.
- **7+ years: up to 1.5 pages** (two pages with the second part-filled), never more than 2.

To fit, first cut the weakest and least relevant bullets, then tighten the layout: margins
down to about 1.5 cm and body text at no less than 10 pt. Never go below that; small text
and cramped pages are harder for people and some applicant tracking systems to read.

Other defaults for Ireland / UK: no photo, no date of birth, a short profile at the top,
reverse-chronological experience, a simple single-column layout (no tables, text boxes or
graphics). Keep their contact details and section order consistent with the master CV unless
they ask otherwise. The summary and skills sections are where tailoring matters most; put
the role's language there where it truthfully fits.

File names: `<Name> - CV - <Company>.docx` and `<Name> - Cover letter - <Company>.docx`.

Do not chase a keyword-match percentage. Applicant tracking systems vary enormously, and
stuffing keywords makes the CV worse for the human who reads it next. Use the JD's language
where it truthfully fits, and stop there.

If the JD asks for something they don't have, leave it out of the CV, list it as a gap in your
summary to the user, and, if it helps, suggest how a cover letter could address it honestly
(adjacent experience, how quickly they'd pick it up).

## Sounding like them, not like AI

Recruiters skim, and machine-sounding CVs get skimmed faster. Every CV and letter should read
as if the user wrote it on a good day.

- Start from their own phrasing in the master CV. Rewrite only what needs to change for this
  role; don't launder the whole document.
- Avoid stock phrases: "spearheaded", "leveraged", "results-driven", "dynamic", "passionate
  about", "proven track record", "synergy", "cutting-edge", "seamlessly", "utilise" (say "use"),
  and piles of adjectives before a noun.
- Vary bullet length and shape. Not every bullet needs to be verb + metric + impact.
- Concrete beats grand: say what they built, ran, fixed or delivered, in plain words.

## The recruiter skim test (before handing over)

Reread the finished CV as a recruiter who sees 200 a day and gives each a few seconds:
- In the top third, would they see this person fits *this* role (title, summary, most
  relevant achievement)? If not, reorder.
- Point to any bullet that says nothing specific ("worked closely with stakeholders",
  "responsible for various projects"). Rewrite it concretely or cut it.
- Anything that sounds templated or AI-written: rewrite it in their voice.
- Does it fit the page rule above?
Fix what this finds before showing the CV.

## Cover letters

1. **Name the role's top three needs first**, from the ad (what it asks for most, or says
   matters most). Write them down for yourself; the letter answers those three.
2. **Open with something specific and true**, never "I am writing to express my interest…"
   or "I am applying for…": a recent project, announcement or the team's remit that
   connects to their experience. A quick web search is fine; cite nothing you can't verify.
   Without something verifiable, open with their most relevant achievement instead.
3. **Middle: one real proof per need**, an achievement from the CV (or confirmed by them)
   mapped to each of the three. Every claim gets its proof; no adjectives about themselves
   without one. If there's an obvious gap the reader will notice, name it briefly and
   honestly.
4. **Close with a specific ask**, not "I look forward to hearing from you": e.g. "I'd welcome
   twenty minutes to walk you through how I rebuilt lead routing at Brightline." Add
   availability (notice period, start date) if the profile has it.
5. **About 200 words; 250 at most.** Addressed to a named person if the listing gives one.
   Match the formality of their field: a hospital research post and a startup ops role should
   not sound the same.

## After writing

Tell the user in 3–5 lines:
- what you emphasised and why (including the employer type you tailored for),
- what you cut,
- gaps you did not paper over,
- numbers you'd add if they can confirm them (the numbers interview, if not done yet),
- anything you'd like them to double-check (a phrasing that stretches what they told you, a
  date you inferred).
