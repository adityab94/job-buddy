# 30-day learning plan

For the top one or two gaps from `jb.py gaps` (or one the user names). The plan's job is to
get them from "missing" to **good enough to interview** in about 30 days, with evidence they
can put on a CV. It is not a course catalogue.

## 1. Pick and justify

Name the skill and why it's first, from the gap report in plain words: "Python is missing in
6 of your 11 scored roles; with it, 4 open roles move up a tier, 3 to great matches." Use the
market counts if setup saved them ("asked for in 12 of 20 Dublin RevOps ads"). One skill per
plan is the default; two only if they're small and related.

## 2. Define "good enough to interview"

Before any resources, write 3–5 concrete things they should be able to do and talk about by
day 30, drawn from how the ads actually use the skill. Specific, testable, interview-shaped:
- Weak: "Know Python."
- Strong: "Write a script that cleans a CRM export (dedupe, fix dates, flag missing owners)
  and explain each step; read and tweak someone else's script; talk through one automation
  you built with it, with the before and after."

Also say plainly what they do *not* need for interviews at this level (e.g. "no need for
classes or web frameworks"), so they don't waste the month.

## 3. Four weeks, each with a milestone

| Week | Focus | Milestone (something they can show) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations they'll actually use | e.g. a first script that reads a CSV and prints a summary |
| 2 | The core skill applied to their own work | e.g. cleaning a real (anonymised) export from their job |
| 3 | The proof project | a working version of the project below |
| 4 | Polish and interview practice | the project written up, plus 3 interview answers using it |

Assume about 4–5 hours a week unless they say otherwise; scale the plan to the time they have.

## 4. One proof project they build themselves

The project is the point: it's what goes on the CV and what they talk about in interviews.
Make it from their own world (their field, their kind of data, a real problem they've had),
small enough for week 3, and honest: it's theirs, built by them. Describe what to build, what
"done" looks like, and how it would read as a CV bullet once finished (written as a future
bullet, not added to their CV until they've done it).

## 5. Free resources, checked

- Prefer official documentation and well-known free providers (the tool's own docs and
  tutorials, freeCodeCamp, Khan Academy, Coursera or edX audit tracks, Microsoft Learn,
  Google's free courses, HSE or professional-body learning portals, public library services).
- **Only link to a page you've opened this session** (web search or fetch) and seen is
  real and free. Otherwise give the resource's name and the site's bare domain only
  ("freeCodeCamp's 'Scientific Computing with Python' course, on freecodecamp.org"). Nothing
  after the domain: not "kaggle.com/learn", not a docs path. Those are guessed links too.
- Two or three resources per week at most. Say which part of each to use and what to skip.
- Note anything that costs money as optional, with the free alternative first.

## 6. After the 30 days

Offer a check-in: when they say they've done it, ask what they built, add the skill and the
project to "Extra experience confirmed by me", re-score the roles that had this gap
(`jb.py gaps` shows them), and update the master CV with them.

Never suggest listing the skill on a CV or in an application before they've actually done
the work.
